# Deadline process-group cleanup

Change-kind: bug fix. Issue #105. Oracle: a timed-out command cannot leave a running member of its owned process group merely because the group leader exits first.

## Behavior and boundary

The deadline wrapper sends SIGTERM to its owned process group, retains the unreaped direct child as an identity anchor through the existing two-second grace period, then sends SIGKILL to remaining group members before reaping the child. A missing group at a signal boundary is benign. Timeout cleanup consumes the full grace period even when SIGTERM already stopped the command. This prevents a reused numeric group identifier from being mistaken for the original workload. Normal child exit propagation, timeout status 124, diagnostics and argument handling remain unchanged. Processes that deliberately create a different process group are outside this helper's ownership boundary; the validation watchdog separately stops the entire owned container.

The new medium regression enters through `main(argv)` with real subprocesses. Its Popen boundary wrapper waits for an owned child PID file, written after SIGTERM is ignored, before returning the real parent process to the runner. This establishes readiness before the actual deadline begins. The parent remains in the runner-created session and group, and exits on SIGTERM; its child remains in that group and ignores SIGTERM. A process-state query observes only the owned PID and fails closed on probe errors. A zombie counts as non-running; the fixture kills the owned group in `finally` and waits for its direct child. The enclosing host watchdog stops the container after either RED or GREEN, retiring remaining fixture state. This is a process-liveness test, not a claim of descendant CPU attribution or a general-purpose process supervisor.

## Review correction: preserve process-group identity

The initial implementation and integration measurements above are historical coordinates. CodeRabbit approved the initial head but its supplementary architecture review identified an actionable identity-reuse race: `process.poll()` reaped the leader before the last group signal. If the original group disappeared and its identifier was reused, cleanup could target an unrelated group. The correction retains the leader until the final group signal, then waits for it. The former 50 ms polling loop is removed. The existing two-second grace is now always consumed on timeout; normal completion still returns immediately with the child's status.

The small `tests/test_deadline_identity.py` regression enters `main(argv)` with a controlled OS/process boundary that reuses the group identifier as soon as the leader is reaped. It observes signals delivered to the unrelated replacement, rather than asserting a private helper call sequence. At head `82c9eb1ea9ee5219a0bf2eb523c040944d71de0f`, its assertion fails with SIGKILL delivered to that replacement. With the corrected source, all four focused deadline cases pass, including the real resistant descendant. Ruff, formatting and Darwin-targeted mypy pass for the changed script and new test. Raw output and source hashes are retained as `identity-results.json` and `identity-inputs.json`. This controlled schedule is not a claim of reproducing PID reuse in the host kernel.

The same stopped reusable worker, validation lock, CPU/memory limits and storage/deadline watchdogs were used. The launch contract was refreshed before execution. The host guard records both successful command completion and successful whole-container termination in `identity-host-resources.json`. Initial test-only lint findings were corrected before the retained final RED/GREEN sequence; no failed behavior run was retried unchanged into success. Earlier broad-suite measurements do not claim to execute this correction; updated hosted CI and current-head review are required before merge.
